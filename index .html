The five needs

Abraham Maslow (1943) proposed that human needs form a hierarchy. Lower needs press hardest until they are met, and then higher needs take over. Marketers write to every level.

Click a level of the pyramid, or use the buttons below.

How the scoring works

This reader uses keyword rules only. There is no AI model behind it, so every point can be traced to a word.

  1. Find indicator words. Each level has a list of words. Strong words add 3 points, medium words 2, and weak words 1.
  2. Find the stated benefit. Phrases that say what the product does for the person, such as “protects my family” or “communicate professional success”, add 4 points to the level they serve. This stops “family” from pulling a Safety message into Social.
  3. Weigh the benefit clause. Words after “helps me”, “so that”, “lets me” or “keep your” get 1 extra point, because the promised benefit shows the motivation.
  4. Pick the primary need. The level with the most points wins. A tie goes to more benefit phrases, then more benefit-clause points, then the lower level, since Maslow said lower needs come first.
  5. Add a secondary need and a confidence label. The next level counts as secondary if it has at least 3 points and at least 40% of the leader’s. Confidence is High when the leader has 6 or more points and leads by 4 or more, Medium when it has 3 or more and leads by 2 or more, and Low otherwise. If nothing matches, the reader says so and does not guess.
